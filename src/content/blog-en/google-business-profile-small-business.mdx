If you run a service business and work locally, a Google Business Profile will do more for you than a Facebook page. It's free, takes an hour and shows up exactly for people who are looking for what you do right now.

Below are instructions on what to set up and in what order.

## What it actually is

Google Business Profile is the panel with your name, phone number, opening hours and reviews that appears on the right of search results and on the map.

For a local business it's the shortest path between someone looking for a service and your phone. A person typing a service and a town name into their phone usually doesn't look through ten pages; they call the first or second number on the list.

## Setting it up step by step

**1. Go to the Google Business Profile page and sign in with a Google account.** Use a business account, not a personal one from fifteen years ago: the profile will stay with you for years and one day you'll hand it over to someone.

**2. Enter the business name.** Exactly as it really is. Don't add keywords to the name.

**3. Choose the primary category.** This is the most important decision in the whole process; I'll come back to it in a moment.

**4. Enter an address or service area.** If customers come to you, give the address. If you travel to them, mark the service area and hide the address.

**5. Add your phone number and website.** The same number as everywhere else.

**6. Verify the profile.** Usually by code, postcard or video. Until it's verified, the profile practically doesn't exist in search results.

<Callout type="warning">
**Don't stuff keywords into the business name.** "Kowalski Plumber Rzeszów Cheap 24h" breaks Google's rules and is a real risk of the profile being suspended. Competitors report cases like this more often than you'd think. The name should be the name.
</Callout>

## Category: the decision that matters most

The primary category determines which searches Google will even consider showing your business for. A badly chosen one can exclude you from half the relevant results.

The rule: **choose the most specific category that describes what you do most often.** Not the broadest, not the most ambitious: the most frequent.

You can add secondary categories, but treat them as extras. Five loosely fitting secondary categories work worse than one well-chosen primary one.

## Photos: the most often skipped thing

Profiles with photos get noticeably more clicks than those without. Yet most small businesses upload one logo and consider the job done.

What's worth adding:

- **Logo:** in good resolution, in a square frame
- **Cover photo:** something representative, not a stock photo
- **Photos of your work:** at least five, ideally a dozen or more
- **Photo of the premises:** entrance, workshop, office, if customers come in
- **Photo of the owner:** a face builds trust faster than any description

Add a few photos every month instead of uploading thirty at once and disappearing for a year. An active profile looks better to Google than a frozen one.

Take photos in portrait or square format: most people view the profile on a phone.

## Reviews: how to collect them without begging

Reviews are one of the strongest factors in local results, and at the same time something almost nobody asks for.

What works:

1. **Ask straight after finishing the job**, when the customer is happy. Not a week later.
2. **Send a direct link** to leave a review. Google generates it in the profile panel. A customer who has to search for it won't write anything.
3. **Ask specifically:** "I'd be grateful if you wrote two sentences about how it went" works better than "please leave a review".
4. **Reply to all of them**, including the good ones. A reply shows the business is alive.

With a negative review, reply calmly, factually and without making excuses. It's not the reviewer reading it, it's your next customers, and they judge how you react.

<Callout type="warning">
Don't buy reviews and don't ask family to leave them from their home address. Google spots patterns, and the penalty is disproportionately harsh compared to the gain. Ten genuine reviews are worth more than fifty bought ones.
</Callout>

## Posts and questions: two features almost nobody uses

**Posts** are short updates shown directly in the profile: a new project, a promotion, a note about holidays. Publishing one post every week or two keeps the profile active.

**Questions and answers** you can ask yourself. It's allowed and sensible: ask the question customers ask most often and answer it straight away from the business account. You get ahead of the phone call and show you have nothing to hide.

## A profile isn't a replacement for a website

The profile answers "where is it and will they pick up the phone". The website answers "can I trust them and what does it cost". They're two different roles and work best together.

A profile without a website is enough in a few situations: a very simple service, a customer who decides in a minute, an obvious price. In most cases, after seeing the profile the customer clicks through to the website to make sure, and decides there.

## FAQ

**Is a Google Business Profile free?**

Yes, setting up and running a Google Business Profile costs nothing. Only ads shown alongside it are paid; the profile itself, photos, reviews and posts are free.

**How long does Google Business Profile verification take?**

It depends on the method. Verification by code or video usually takes a few days, by postcard up to two weeks. Until it's confirmed the profile practically doesn't appear in results, so do it straight away when setting up.

**How do I choose a category for my Google Business Profile?**

Choose the most specific category describing what you do most often, not the broadest possible. The primary category decides which searches Google will even consider showing your business for. Treat secondary categories as extras, not a wish list.

**How do I collect Google reviews?**

Ask right after finishing a job and send the customer a direct link to leave a review, generated in the profile panel. Reply to all reviews, including positive ones. Don't buy reviews: Google detects these patterns and the penalty can be severe.

**Is a Google Business Profile enough instead of a website?**

It's enough for very simple services where the customer decides immediately and the price is obvious. In most cases, after viewing the profile the customer visits the website anyway to check the scope and credibility, and only decides there.

**How do I get my profile to show higher?**

Complete the profile in full, choose a narrow primary category, collect genuine reviews, add photos regularly and make sure your name, address and phone number are identical on your website, your profile and elsewhere online. These four things do more than any tricks.
