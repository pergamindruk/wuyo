This post is for business owners who paid for a website, type their trade and town into Google, and don't see it. Not on the first page of results, not on the third.

## First check whether Google knows your website at all

Type into Google: `site:yourdomain.pl` (no space after the colon, with your address).

- **A list of pages shows up.** Google knows the site, it just doesn't show it for the words you type. Go to reasons 4–7.
- **Nothing shows up.** Google doesn't know the site or isn't allowed in. Start with reasons 1–3.

That one search tells you where to start.

## Why isn't my website on Google: the 7 most common reasons

### 1. The website is new

Google doesn't see a site on the day it goes live. It usually takes from a few days to a few weeks. If the site has been up for a week, that's not a problem yet. You can speed things up, though, see reason 3.

### 2. The website is blocked from search engines

While building a site, the contractor turns on a block so Google doesn't show the unfinished version. Then the site launches and nobody remembers the block.

<CheckItem>WordPress: Dashboard, then Settings, Reading. The box “Discourage search engines from indexing this site” must be unticked</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Other websites: type `yourdomain.pl/robots.txt` into the browser. If you see the line `Disallow: /`, the whole site is closed to search engines</CheckItem>

If you're not sure what you're looking at, send those two screenshots to whoever built the site. The fix takes a few minutes.

### 3. Nobody submitted the website to Google

Google will find the site eventually, but it's faster if you show it. The free tool for that is Google Search Console. You add your domain and submit the sitemap, a file listing all your pages.

Search Console also shows which pages Google already knows, and which it won't add and why. That's the place to start when your site doesn't show up in results.

### 4. You're searching for different words than the ones on the site

The site can be on Google, but for words nobody types. Example: the home page has only the business name and the slogan “Quality since 1998”. A customer types “plumber Rzeszów” and Google has no reason to show them your site.

Each main service should have its own page with the service and town in the title and the first heading. That's why for service businesses I usually recommend a few pages rather than one long one. More on that choice in [one-page or multi-page website](/en/blog/one-page-or-multi-page-website).

### 5. The text is hidden in images

Google reads text. If your whole offer is pasted in as an image or a PDF of your leaflet, the site is almost empty to a search engine. The same goes for a site with just photos and a phone number.

A few paragraphs of plain text on each page are enough: what you do, for whom, where, roughly what it costs.

### 6. The website works badly on phones

Google looks mainly at the mobile version. If the text falls apart on a phone, the buttons are too small and the page loads slowly, it loses to competitors who got this right. I described a few of these symptoms in [is your business website losing customers](/en/blog/is-your-business-website-losing-customers).

### 7. No Google Business Profile

For searches with a town, for example “mechanic Rzeszów”, Google shows a map of businesses above the regular results. You don't get onto that map through your website, but through your Google Business Profile. Without it you hand the best spot to competitors, even if your website is fine. How to set it up is in [Google Business Profile for a small business](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business).

<Callout type="info">
Have you ever changed your website address or moved to a new domain? Check that the old addresses redirect to the new ones. If they don't, Google may keep showing pages that no longer exist and treat the new ones as strangers.
</Callout>

## Checked everything and still nothing?

Then the problem usually sits deeper: in how the site is built, in the code, or in the fact that nobody has touched it for years. Sometimes fixing the titles and adding text is enough. ## FAQ

**How long until a new website shows up on Google?**

Usually from a few days to a few weeks. Submitting the site and sitemap in Google Search Console speeds it up. Showing up on Google doesn't yet mean ranking high, that's a separate matter.

**Do I have to pay Google to be in the results?**

No. Regular results are free. You only pay for ads, the results marked as sponsored.

**People find me by business name, but not by service. Why?**

Because your business name is on the site, and the service with the town probably isn't. Add the service and town to the page title, the first heading and the text. Ideally a separate page for each main service.

**Can I check myself whether the site is blocked?**

Yes. In WordPress, look at Settings, Reading. For other sites, open `yourdomain.pl/robots.txt` and look for the line `Disallow: /`. If you see it, write to whoever built the site.
