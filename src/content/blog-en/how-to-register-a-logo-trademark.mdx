How do you protect your business logo? You file it as a trademark with the Polish Patent Office (UPRP), ideally online, for the specific industries you work in. It sounds like a job for a lawyer, but a simple application is something you can handle yourself.

Why bother? Picture this. You spend five years building a brand in your area. People know your sign from the van and the shopfront. Then one day a company from the other end of Poland registers a similar logo in the same industry and sends you a letter telling you to stop using yours. Without a registration, defending yourself is hard.

Here are five steps, in the order worth doing them.

## Step 1. Make sure the logo is yours

Before you file anything, check your contract with the designer. It has to transfer the economic copyright, with "registration as a trademark" listed as a field of use. Without that, you're filing something that legally isn't yours, and the registration can be invalidated later.

Be careful with logos built from templates and stock graphics, too. Some services forbid registering their elements as a trademark. I cover the details in [logo copyright](/en/blog/logo-copyright).

## Step 2. Check nobody already has a similar mark

The office won't reject your application just because a similar mark exists. But the owner of that mark can file an opposition. Then you lose time and fees.

You can check this yourself, for free:

- **The Polish Patent Office search:** marks filed in Poland.
- **TMview:** marks from across the European Union in one place.

Search by name and by similar sound. "Budmax" and "Bud-Maks" in the same industry are almost the same thing to the office.

## Step 3. Choose the classes of goods and services

This is the most important decision in the whole application. A mark protects you only in the industries you list. All goods and services are split into 45 classes (the Nice Classification).

Examples:

- Renovation company: class 37, building and repair services.
- Bakery selling bread: class 30.
- Beauty salon: class 44.
- Online clothing shop: class 25 for clothes and 35 for retail.

Each extra class costs extra. Pick the ones you really work in, or plan to in the next few years.

## Step 4. File and pay

You file online, on the Patent Office website. You need:

- a good quality file of the logo, exactly in the version you want to protect,
- a list of goods and services from the chosen classes,
- your business details,
- an electronic signature or a Trusted Profile (profil zaufany).

Official fees in Poland (as of November 2026, check the current table at uprp.gov.pl): an online application in one class is 400 PLN (450 PLN on paper), each further class 120 PLN. Once the right is granted, you also pay for 10 years of protection: 400 PLN per class. With one class that comes to about 800 PLN for a decade.

If you sell, or plan to sell, in several countries, you can file straight away for the European Union. It costs more, but one application covers every EU country.

## Step 5. Wait for the decision

After you file, the office examines the application and publishes it. From publication, others have 3 months to oppose. If nobody does and everything is in order, you get a decision granting the right. The whole thing usually takes a few months.

The filing date is what counts. If someone files a similar mark a day after you, you have priority, and the 10 years of protection also run from that day. So don't put it off.

## How to register your logo so you don't file it twice

The most common mistake: filing a logo that won't be around a year later. If the mark was made in a hurry, has five colours or doesn't work in black and white, sooner or later you'll change it. And a new version means a new application and new fees.

## FAQ

**Can I use the ® sign with my logo?**

Only after registration. Before that, using ® misleads customers and can get you fined. Before registration you can use ™, which only says you treat the mark as yours.

**Does registering my business in CEIDG protect my logo?**

No. An entry in CEIDG or KRS protects the business name to a limited extent, and the logo not at all. Only registration with the Patent Office protects the mark.

**Should I register the name alone or the logo with the name?**

A word mark protects the name itself, in any font and colour. A figurative mark protects the logo in a specific look. If your name is strong and unusual, start with a word mark. With a descriptive name like "Remonty Rzeszów", a figurative mark is what's left.

**How long does protection last?**

10 years from the filing date. After that you can renew it for further ten-year periods, with no limit.
