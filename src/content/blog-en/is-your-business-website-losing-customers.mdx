Short answer: take your phone, open your website and check the seven things on the list below. It'll take 10 minutes. If three or more don't work, your site is probably losing customers and you don't know it.

I regularly look through local business websites in Rzeszów and the area. Most of them aren't ugly. They were simply made a few years ago and nobody has checked them since. But customers check them every day, on their phones, in the queue at the till.

## 1. Does the website open at all

It sounds silly, but it's the most common problem. The domain expired, the server stopped working, someone changed hosting and the site got stuck halfway. The customer sees an error and calls the next business on the list.

**How to check:** type the address on your phone, not on the computer where it's saved. Then search for your business in Google and click the link in the results.

## 2. Is there a padlock next to the address

The browser shows a padlock next to the address when the site has an SSL certificate. Without it, Chrome shows "Not secure". A customer who sees that won't fill in the form, and some people close the page straight away.

**How to check:** look at the address bar. The address should start with `https://`, not `http://`.

## 3. Can it be read on a phone

Most people look for local businesses on their phones. If they have to zoom with their fingers, scroll sideways or hit tiny buttons, they give up.

**How to check:** open the site on your phone and try to read the offer without zooming. Buttons should be big enough to hit with a thumb.

## 4. Can the phone number be tapped

A customer on a phone doesn't want to copy your number. They want to tap and call. If the number is an image or plain text, some people won't bother.

**How to check:** tap the number on your website. The phone should offer to call straight away.

## 5. Does it load in a few seconds

People don't wait. If a page takes longer than 3–4 seconds, a large share will close it before they see anything. The most common cause is huge photos uploaded straight from the camera.

**How to check:** turn off Wi-Fi on your phone and open the site on mobile data. Count in your head how many seconds until you see content.

## 6. What's in the footer

Look at the bottom of the page. If you see "© 2017", customers see it too and think: "are they still in business?" Same with a news section whose last post is three years old, and a price list with old prices.

**How to check:** scroll to the very bottom. Check the year, address, opening hours and phone number. Everything must match your Google Business Profile.

## 7. How the site looks in Google

In search results Google shows the page title and a short description under it. If it says "Home page", "Home" or the name of a template, customers don't know what you do and click on a competitor whose title says "Mechanic Rzeszów: repairs and inspections".

**How to check:** type your business name into Google and see what title and description appear. Then type your service and town, e.g. "hairdresser Rzeszów", and check whether you're on the first page at all.

## FAQ

**How do I check whether a website works on a phone?**

The easiest way is to open it on your own phone and try to read the offer without zooming. If you see a shrunken desktop version, the site isn't phone-friendly.

**What does "Not secure" next to the address mean?**

The site doesn't have an SSL certificate. Data entered in the form isn't encrypted and the browser warns the customer about it. A certificate can usually be added in your hosting settings, often for free.

**Can an old website be fixed instead of building a new one?**

Often, yes. Small things like a tappable number, an up-to-date footer or an SSL certificate are quick to fix. If the site doesn't work on phones and loads very slowly, a new one usually works out cheaper.
