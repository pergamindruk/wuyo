## Vector or PNG: the one difference that matters

All logo files fall into two groups.

<GreenRed
  green="Vector (SVG, PDF, EPS, AI): the logo is saved as shapes and lines. You can enlarge it from a business card to a 5-metre banner and the edges stay sharp. Print shops, sign makers and garment printers ask for exactly this file."
  red="Pixel graphics (PNG, JPG): the logo is saved as a grid of tiny squares. It looks fine on screen. Enlarged, the edges turn jagged and blurry. You won't make a decent shop sign from a small JPG."
/>

A simple rule: the vector is the "master" file. You can always make a PNG of any size from it. Not the other way round: then the logo has to be redrawn.

## Which logo files you should get from a designer

This is the minimum. If anything is missing, ask before you pay the rest.

<CheckItem>**SVG**: a vector for your website and for further design work</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>**PDF**: a vector for the print shop, the easiest to open without special software</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>**PNG with a transparent background**: for the web, presentations and Word documents</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>**JPG**: as a fallback, where a system won't accept other files</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>**Horizontal and vertical versions**: horizontal for the website header and email signature, vertical for square spaces such as a profile picture</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>**Single-colour versions, black and white**: for engraving, stamps and printing on dark backgrounds</CheckItem>

It's also good to know which colours the logo uses (colour numbers for print and screen) and which typeface is in the name. A brand book usually covers this. What exactly goes into one is in [brand book: what it is and what it costs](/en/blog/brand-book-guidelines-cost).

## Which file for what: a cheat sheet

Save or print it. It'll come in handy next time someone asks for the logo "in good quality".

| Where you use the logo | Which file to send |
|---|---|
| Print shop: business cards, flyers, posters | PDF or SVG |
| Banner, shop sign, window or vehicle graphics | PDF or SVG |
| T-shirt printing, embroidery | PDF or SVG |
| Website | SVG, or PNG |
| Facebook, Instagram, Google Business Profile | PNG |
| Email signature | PNG |
| Presentation, offer in Word | PNG with a transparent background |

If the print shop sends a file back with comments, it's usually about a missing vector, wrong colours or no bleed. More on that in [how to prepare print-ready files](/en/blog/how-to-prepare-print-ready-files).

## All you have is a JPG from Facebook? What you can do

It's a common situation. The logo was made by a friend, a nephew or a company that no longer exists. All that's left is what's online.

- **Search your old email.** Type "logo", "pdf", "svg" or the designer's name into your mailbox search. The files are often sitting in an attachment from years ago.
- **Ask the author.** If you have a contract with a transfer of rights, you also have grounds to ask for the source files. How to check this is covered in [logo copyright](/en/blog/logo-copyright).
- **Have the logo redrawn as a vector.** A designer recreates the mark from the image. For a simple logo it's a short job billed by the hour: with me 100 PLN net per hour plus VAT. For a logo with lots of detail, a refresh of the mark is sometimes the more sensible option. A refresh of an existing logo costs from 1,000 PLN net with me.

One warning. Many "free converters" turn a JPG into an SVG file, but inside there's still a pixel image or a jagged outline. The file name is right, the quality isn't.

## FAQ

**Should logo files be included in the design price?**

Yes. The file package is part of the design, not an extra. With me SVG, PDF, PNG and JPG are included in the logo price, from 890 PLN net plus 23% VAT.

**How can I check whether my logo is a vector?**

Open the file and zoom right in, say to 800%. If the edges stay smooth, it's a vector. If you see steps and blur, it's a pixel image. The file extension (.pdf, .svg) alone isn't proof, because there may be a pasted image inside.

**Which logo file should I send to the print shop?**

PDF or SVG. If the print shop asks for AI or EPS, ask your designer to prepare them. They can be saved from the source file without redrawing the logo.

**Can I make a banner or shop sign from a PNG?**

Only if the PNG is very large and the logo is simple. In most cases it's better to have the logo redrawn as a vector first. That costs less than a banner that turns out blurry once it's hung.
