I often hear: "I have Facebook, people message me there, why do I need a website?" It's a good question. Facebook isn't bad. The problem is it does something different from a website. Below I explain what, without the theory.

## Facebook and a website are two different tools

Facebook works when someone already knows you. A neighbour shares a post, a regular customer sees a promotion, someone in a group asks for a recommendation. It's a channel to people who are close.

A website works when someone **doesn't know you** but is looking for exactly what you do right now. They type "plumber Rzeszów" or "communion catering Rzeszów" into Google and choose from what they're shown.

If all your work comes from recommendations and you don't want more, Facebook may be enough. If you want new customers from the area, you need a place Google shows.

## What Facebook won't give you

**It isn't yours.** Your profile sits on someone else's platform. They change the rules, block your account, limit your reach, and you can't do anything about it. Your website and domain are in your name.

**Posts disappear.** A good post from six months ago is buried deep in history. Nobody scrolls that far. On a website your offer, prices and projects are always on top.

**Not everyone has an account.** Some customers don't have Facebook or don't want to log in to see your offer. Especially businesses looking for a subcontractor.

**It's hard to keep things organised.** On a profile you can't build a clear offer split into services, a form with specific questions or a page for each service. And those are exactly the pages Google shows for specific searches.

## When Facebook alone is enough

Not everyone needs a website straight away. Facebook is enough when:

## When you need a website

<CheckItem>Customers search for your service in Google: "mechanic", "hairdresser", "renovations" plus a town name</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>You keep answering the same questions: how much, what it looks like, where you are</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>You want to show your work and prices in one place, not across 40 posts</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>You work with businesses that check whether you're serious before talking</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Competitors in the area have websites and you don't</CheckItem>

If you ticked two or more, a website will start paying for itself.

## How to combine them in practice

Example: you run a car workshop. On Facebook you post photos of repairs, free slots before winter, changed hours over the holidays. On the website you have the permanent things: a list of services, approximate prices, a map, phone number and form.

Someone searches Google for "tyre change Rzeszów". They land on your site. They see the offer and call. Later they like your profile and come back with the next fault. One drives the other.

## FAQ

**Does a small business need a website?**

If you want new customers from Google, yes. If all your work comes from recommendations and your calendar is full, you can wait. A free Google Business Profile is enough to start with.

**Does a Facebook page show up in Google?**

Sometimes, but usually only when someone types the business name. For general searches like "service plus town", Google more often shows business websites and Google Business Profiles.
