The website should let a new client find you on their own and understand straight away what you can make.

## Who actually visits a manufacturer's website

A shoe shop's website is visited by someone who wants to buy shoes. A manufacturer's website is most often visited by a purchasing manager, the owner of another business, or an engineer who has a part drawing and is looking for someone to make it.

That person wants answers to three questions, and slogans don't give them:

1. Can they make this?
2. Can they handle my quantity?
3. What's the fastest way to send them an enquiry?

If the site only has a photo of the hall and the line "high quality, competitive prices", that person closes the tab and opens the next Google result.

## A website for a manufacturing company: what it must include

Below is the list I start from with every site like this. Not everything has to be there on day one, but each point answers a question the client will ask on the phone anyway.

<CheckItem>What you make. Specifically: "custom wooden stairs", "turned parts in steel and aluminium", not "a wide range of services"</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>The materials you work with and the sizes you can handle</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Your machinery. A list with machine names and what you make on them is enough</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>The minimum order quantity and whether you make single prototypes</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Certificates and standards, if you have them. As text and as a downloadable file</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Photos of products and the hall. Phone photos in good light are better than someone else's from the internet</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Where you deliver. Your own transport, shipping across Poland, abroad</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A quote request form with space for an attachment</CheckItem>

Each product or service is a good candidate for its own page. A client types "laser sheet cutting Rzeszów" or "custom office furniture" into Google and lands exactly where they should. That's why for manufacturers I usually recommend a site with several pages rather than one long one. I explained the difference in [one-page or multi-page website](/en/blog/one-page-or-multi-page-website).

## A quote form that saves phone calls

This is the most important part of a manufacturer's website. A plain "name, email, message" form ends with you calling the client and asking about everything again.

A better form asks straight away for what you need to give a price:

- what the enquiry is about (chosen from a list of your services),
- material and number of pieces,
- the date the client needs it by,
- an attachment: a drawing, sketch, photo or file with dimensions.

The enquiry lands in your inbox with all the details. You can price it right away or pass the file to your production engineer.

</Callout>

## An English or German version

If you sell abroad or want to start, a second language version makes sense. A foreign purchasing manager rarely translates websites in the browser. More often they pick a company that speaks their language from the start.

You don't have to translate everything. To start with, the home page, the product list, the machinery and the form are enough. I price the second language version separately, because it depends on the number of pages. You or a translator provide the text, and I add it and set it up for search engines.

## Does a manufacturing company need an online shop?

Usually not. Most manufacturers price each order separately, so a basket with prices doesn't make sense. A quote request form works better here.

A shop makes sense when you have standard products at fixed prices, for example ready-made pallets, planters or fence elements, and want to sell them to retail customers too. A WooCommerce shop with up to 30 products costs from 6,900 PLN net with me. Details are in [how much an online shop costs](/en/blog/online-shop-cost).

## FAQ

**How much does a website for a manufacturing company cost?**

With me, a business website with up to 5 pages costs 2,990 PLN net, and a one-page site from 1,990 PLN net. Plus 23% VAT. The domain and hosting for the first year are included.

**What should I prepare before ordering a website for my plant?**

A list of products and services, a list of machines, photos of products and the hall, certificates, company details and your logo. The full list is in [what to prepare for a website](/en/blog/what-to-prepare-for-a-website).

**Can I show products I made for clients on the website?**

Only with their consent. Many plants manufacture to order from someone else's drawings and have signed a confidentiality agreement. In that case it's better to show photos of the machines, the process and parts without the client's markings.

**Will a website help me find clients beyond referrals?**

It can, if it has separate pages for what people search for in Google and a well set up [Google Business Profile](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business). A page with slogans and a phone number won't do that on its own.
