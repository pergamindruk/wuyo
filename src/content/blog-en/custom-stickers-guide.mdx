A sticker is the cheapest branded item there is. For 89 PLN you get 50 of them, and they end up on parcels, packaging, folders and car doors. No other material gives that much reach for that money.

Below is everything you need to decide before ordering, and what isn't worth overpaying for. Prices in PLN, net.

## Paper or vinyl: one decision that changes the price

This is the first question and the most important.

**Paper:** cheaper, takes colour well, but can't handle moisture. It goes soggy in the rain and peels off in the fridge.

**Vinyl:** a quarter more expensive, waterproof, survives washing, frost and sun.

| Use | Material |
|---|---|
| Sealing parcels and boxes | paper |
| Stickers for folders, notebooks, company materials | paper |
| Labels for jars, bottles, cosmetics | vinyl |
| Stickers for equipment, tools, crates | vinyl |
| Anything outdoors: car, shop window, sign | vinyl |
| Food products kept in the fridge | vinyl |

<Callout type="warning">
The most common mistake: labels for jars of preserves printed on paper. A jar taken out of the fridge gets covered in condensation, the paper soaks it up and the label comes off in flakes. For food products vinyl isn't a luxury, it's a requirement.
</Callout>

## Shape and size

**Round** is the default choice and copes best with cutting: there are no corners to bend and peel. Diameters of 4–6 cm work for most uses.

**Rectangular and square** fit more content, so they suit labels with a product description, ingredients or a date.

**Contour-cut:** any shape, cut along the outline of the design. Looks the best, costs more. Makes sense for a logo with a distinctive shape.

A practical tip: a sticker smaller than 3 cm becomes unreadable if it has anything on it besides the mark itself. If you want to fit a website and phone number, start at 5 cm.

## How to prepare the design

Stickers have one feature that's easy to forget: they're cut, and cutting has a bigger tolerance than trimming paper with a guillotine.

<CheckItem>Background extending as bleed, at least 3 mm beyond the cut line</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Content at least 4–5 mm inside the cut line</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A vector file or a 300 dpi raster at the final size</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Colours in CMYK</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>For a contour-cut sticker, a separate cut path in the file</CheckItem>
<CheckItem ok={false}>A thin frame along the edge of the sticker: with the slightest shift in the cut you can see it's crooked</CheckItem>

That last one surprises people most often. A frame around the edge looks great in the design and terrible after cutting, because on one side it comes out half a millimetre wider. If you really want a frame, give it at least 4 mm of space from the edge.

I wrote more about bleed, CMYK and resolution in [how to prepare a print-ready file](/en/blog/how-to-prepare-print-ready-files).

## Product labels: what else to remember

If the sticker is a label for a product on sale, extra requirements come in that don't apply to an ordinary company sticker.

For food and cosmetics you need, among other things, the product name, ingredients, producer details, weight or volume and the use-by or best-before date. What's required depends on the product category, so confirm the list within your industry or with whoever handles your formalities; I'm responsible for fitting everything in legibly, not for legal compliance.

A practical design tip: leave **more space on the label than seems necessary**. The ingredients always turn out longer than in the first version, and the date has to be printed or written on after the order.

## When stickers are a better choice than business cards

An unusual approach, but it works in a few industries.

If you leave a physical trace behind (an installed device, repaired equipment, a completed installation), a sticker with your logo and phone number stuck on the spot works for years. A business card goes into a drawer on the day of the visit and stays there.

Service engineers, installers, fitting companies and workshops usually get more out of stickers than business cards. Best of all is one with the other: a business card in the hand, a sticker on the equipment.

If you're ordering stickers together with other materials, also check [how much business card printing costs](/en/blog/business-card-printing-cost): in one order it comes out cheaper than separately.

## FAQ

**How much do 50 logo stickers cost?**

Printing 50 stickers from a ready file is 89 PLN, 25% more on waterproof vinyl. With a design from scratch the set comes to 219 PLN. Unusual shapes are quoted individually.

**Are paper stickers waterproof?**

No. Paper soaks up water and the label comes off, including from condensation on a jar taken out of the fridge. If the sticker comes into contact with moisture, you need vinyl. It adds 25% to the print price.

**What's the minimum sticker run?**

With me there's no strict minimum; I usually quote from 20–50 pieces. I print in-house, so the minimum runs of print shops working on shared sheets don't apply.

**What size sticker should I choose?**

For just the logo, 3–4 cm is enough. If it also needs a phone number or website, start at 5–6 cm. For product labels the size follows from how much content you need to fit, so first write down everything that has to be on it, then choose the format.

**Can I order stickers in an unusual shape?**

Yes, with contour cutting the shape can be anything: following the logo outline or a designed path. It costs more than a standard shape, so for simple designs I usually advise against it, while for distinctive logos it's worth it.

**How long does it take?**

With a ready file usually 1–3 working days; with a design from scratch add time for the design and your approval. I agree the exact date with the quote.
