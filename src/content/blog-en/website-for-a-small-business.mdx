A small business website doesn't have to be big or expensive. It has one job: get the person who found you to call or send a message.

Below is what such a site needs, and what isn't worth paying for.

## What a small business website needs

### 1. A phone number you can see right away

Most people open a small business website on their phone. Put the number at the top and make it call with one tap. If a customer has to hunt for your contact details, they close the page and call the next company on the list.

### 2. Specific services, not vague promises

"A wide range of services" tells nobody anything. Say what you do: "timing belt replacement", "full bathroom renovation", "bookkeeping for sole traders". One or two sentences per service. If you can, give a starting price. People who know the ballpark are more likely to call. For an example with a treatment price list, see the post on a [website for a beauty salon](/en/blog/website-for-a-beauty-salon).

### 3. Real photos

Stock photos are easy to spot. Your own photos work better: the premises, the team on the job, before and after shots. A decent phone and a day with good light are enough. For restaurants, photos of real dishes work best, as I explain in the post on a [website for a restaurant](/en/blog/website-for-a-restaurant).

### 4. Customer reviews

The simplest option is to show your Google reviews. If you aren't collecting them yet, start with your [Google Business Profile](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business). Three or four real reviews with names do more than the best text about your company.

### 5. Where you work

Address, map, and the towns you cover. This helps customers and Google alike. Without it, it's hard to show up for searches like "service + town".

### 6. A short contact form

Name, phone or email, and one message field. Every extra field means fewer enquiries. A form collects personal data, so you also need a privacy policy.

### 7. Fast on a phone

The page has to load in a few seconds and look right on a small screen. Check it yourself: open your site on your phone using mobile data, not Wi-Fi.

### 8. The basics for Google

Every page has its own title and description, and the text names the service and the town. Add a Google Business Profile that links to the site. This is routine work I do on every website.

## What a small business doesn't need

- **Sliders at the top.** Hardly anyone looks at the third slide. One good photo and one sentence about what you do work better.
- **A blog from day one.** A blog makes sense once you have time to write regularly. Get the website right first, then add articles if you want.
- **A long company history.** Three sentences on who you are and how long you've been in business are enough.
- **Ten subpages.** With 1–3 services, one longer page is enough. With more services, separate subpages work better. I cover this in [one page or multi-page website](/en/blog/one-page-or-multi-page-website).

## How to check your current website

Take your phone and visit your site the way a customer would. Ask yourself three questions:

1. Can I tell within 5 seconds what this company does and where it works?
2. Can I call with one tap?
3. Can I see at least one review and one real photo?

If any answer is "no", the site is losing customers. It doesn't always need rebuilding. Sometimes fixing the top of the page and adding contact details is enough.

## Frequently asked questions

**Isn't a Facebook page enough?**
It can be enough to start with, but Facebook doesn't show up well in Google, and your reach depends on Facebook, not on you. See the comparison in [website or Facebook page](/en/blog/website-or-facebook-page).

**Will I be able to change things myself?**
Yes, if you choose WordPress. The price is the same, and you get a one-hour recorded training on the admin panel so you can go back to it.

**Can I get a website for a few hundred złoty?**
You can, but it usually means a ready-made template with no work on the content and no Google setup. What to watch out for is in [cheap website: what to watch out for](/en/blog/cheap-website-what-to-watch-out-for).

**How long until the first enquiries come in?**
It depends on your industry and local competition. A Google Business Profile linked to your site usually brings the first calls fastest.
