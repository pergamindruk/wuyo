I hear this question a lot from people starting a business or finally wanting to "sort out the internet". There's money for one thing, but both are needed. Below is how to plan it sensibly so you don't pay twice.

## Why the logo usually comes first

A website uses the logo at every step. Button colours, headings, the typeface, the icon in the browser tab: all of it follows from your mark.

If you build a website without a logo and the logo arrives six months later, there are two ways forward:

- the website keeps its old colours and doesn't match the new logo,
- or it has to be reworked, which means paying for part of the work twice.

A real-life example: a renovation company builds a website in navy and orange "for now". Then it orders a logo, and the designer suggests green because it suits the industry better. Now there's a choice: a logo designed around the old website, or changes to the website. Both are worse than doing it the other way round.

What a logo alone costs and what the price depends on is covered in [how much does a logo cost](/en/blog/logo-design-cost).

<Callout type="info">
A logo made before the website adds nothing to the bill. It makes every later project faster instead: the website, business cards, shop sign and posts all have ready colours and a typeface.
</Callout>

## Logo or website: when the order flips

There are situations where the website should come first. Most often three:

1. **Customers are searching for you on Google right now.** There's demand for your service in your area, but you're not in the results. Every month without a website means enquiries going to your competitors.
2. **You already have a mark that "sort of works".** It isn't perfect, but it exists as a vector file and people recognise it. Then the website can be built on it, and the logo refreshed later.
3. **You work under your own name.** A photographer, accountant or coach: here a well-chosen typeface with your name is often enough instead of a graphic mark.

If you're not sure you need a website at all, or whether a Google Business Profile would do, see [Google Business Profile for a small business](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business). It's free and often brings the first phone calls.

## What not to do

- **Don't make a logo "quickly" just so the website has one.** A logo rarely changes and appears everywhere: on invoices, the van, the shop sign. Whether an AI logo is enough to start with is covered in [AI logo or graphic designer](/en/blog/ai-logo-or-graphic-designer).
- **Don't give the developer just the logo file.** If someone other than the designer builds the website, also hand over the colours, typefaces and rules for using the mark. Without them the developer picks the rest, and the website looks like it belongs to a different business than your business card.
- **Don't put both off forever.** Better to do one thing properly now than two things perfectly "some day".

## FAQ

**What comes first: the logo or the website?**

In most cases the logo. The website uses its colours and typeface, so a logo made later means changes to the website. The exceptions: you already have a mark as a vector file, or customers are searching for you on Google right now.

**How much do a logo and a website cost together?**

With me the Firma w Internecie package costs 3,490 PLN net + 23% VAT. It includes a logo, mini brand book, 150 printed business cards, an email signature, a website with up to 5 pages, and the domain and hosting for the first year. Marka Start and the website bought separately would cost 4,480 PLN net.

**Can I have a website without a logo?**

You can, if you work under your own name or already have a mark you want to keep. Then I build the website on the name itself, set in a well-chosen typeface. When the logo comes later, swapping it in is simple, but the colours may need adjusting.

**Is it a problem if one designer does the logo and someone else does the website?**

It doesn't have to be, as long as the second person gets the logo files and the rules: colours, typefaces, spacing. Without them the website often drifts away from the rest of your business materials.
