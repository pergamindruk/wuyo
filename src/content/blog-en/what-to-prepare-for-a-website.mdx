This post isn't about which style to choose. It's about what to have at hand before you contact a designer. ## Why prepare this before the conversation

If you come with just "I want a website", I first have to ask about everything in turn. You look for photos, I wait. You write text in the evenings, I wait. The site stands still while you pay for a domain with nothing on it.

If you come with a ready folder, I know straight away how many pages are needed, whether a one-page site is enough and what it will cost. You get a quote faster and your website faster.

## Text: the most common cause of delays

Most business owners say: "I'll write the text at the end." Then the site is ready, and where the service descriptions should be there's an empty template.

You don't have to write like a journalist. Answer three questions for each service:

- What exactly do you do?
- For whom?
- Why should a customer choose you over the business down the street?

An example. Instead of "We offer top-quality renovation services", write: "Bathroom renovations in Rzeszów and the surrounding area. There are two of us, we're on site every day, and we clean up after work." The first sentence says nothing. The second tells the customer what to expect.

I adapt the text and page structure for search engines, so don't worry about keywords. You give me the facts about your business, I arrange them so Google understands what you do and where.

## Photos: better from a phone than from a stock library

Smiling people in suits from a stock library are obvious from a mile away. Customers want to see you, your workshop, your company van, the results of your work.

A good phone is enough if you remember a few things:

- take photos in daylight, by a window or outdoors,
- hold the phone horizontally,
- tidy the frame: cables, boxes and mugs show up more on a website than in real life,
- take several shots of the same thing, I'll pick the best.

Have photos of your work from recent years? Put them all in one folder. I'll take care of choosing and cropping.

## Details people forget

The same five questions come up with every business website: the exact address, opening hours, the number customers should call, the email for the contact form and a link to your Google Business Profile.

These details must be identical on the website, on your Google Business Profile and in business directories. A different address format in each place tells Google something doesn't add up. How to set up your profile is covered in [Google Business Profile for a small business](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business).

If you already have a domain, check where it's registered and whether you have the login. This often only comes up when connecting the website. No domain? The domain and server for the first year are included in the price of every website I build. How to pick a good name is in [how to choose a domain name for your business](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-domain-name).
