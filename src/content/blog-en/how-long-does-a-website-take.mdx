## Stages: from the first message to the site going live

Here's how it works with me, in order:

1. **Conversation and quote.** You tell me what you do and what you need. I ask about your services, service area and what you already have.
2. **Offer.** You get the scope, price and timeline in writing.
3. **Contract and a 50% deposit.** From this point I reserve time for your website.
4. **Materials.** Logo, text, photos, business details. From the day they're complete, I count 7–14 working days.
5. **Design and build.** I lay out the pages, adapt the content for Google, set up the form and map.
6. **Preview and revisions.** You see the finished site before anyone else does. You tell me what to change.
7. **Launch and final payment.** The site goes live at your address and you get the login details.

Stages 1–3 can take a day or a week. It depends on how quickly you make decisions.

## What slows down work on a website the most

### Missing text

The most common cause of delays. "I'll write the text at the end" ends with a finished site and an empty template where the service descriptions should be. You don't need to write beautifully. A few sentences per service are enough: what you do, for whom and why to choose you. The full list of materials is in [what to prepare for a website](/en/blog/what-to-prepare-for-a-website).

### No logo

If you don't have a logo, it has to be made first. A logo design takes me 7–10 working days from brief approval. Only then is it clear which colours and typefaces go on the site. That's why for a new business it's better to order the Firma w Internecie package straight away. The logo and website are then planned together, not one after the other with a break to find a contractor.

### Revisions in instalments

One list of comments in one email is one day of work. Five emails over two weeks, including changes of mind about things already approved, are two weeks of delay. Collect your comments, show the site to your partner or family straight away, and send everything at once.

### A domain without a login

Already have a web address but don't know where it's registered or who has the password? That can hold up the launch for a few days. Check it at the very start.

## Can it be faster?

Yes, if the materials are ready at the start and the site has no unusual features. A one-page site has less content to prepare than a site with five pages, so it usually lands closer to the lower end of the timeline.

Cutting corners doesn't always pay, though. A rushed website without thought-through text looks nice but doesn't bring customers from Google. I'd rather take two weeks and deliver a site that works than one week and then a month of fixes.

A warning about very short deadlines: if someone promises a website by tomorrow for a few hundred PLN, it's usually a ready template with your logo swapped in. What to watch out for with such offers is in [cheap website: what to watch out for](/en/blog/cheap-website-what-to-watch-out-for).

## After launch

A live website isn't the end. Google needs time to find it and start showing it in results. A new website is rarely on the first page of results on launch day. A well set-up [Google Business Profile](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business) with the same address and phone number as the website helps.
