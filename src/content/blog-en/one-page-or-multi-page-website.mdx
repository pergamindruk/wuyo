If you offer 4 or more services, or want customers to find you in Google for each of them separately, choose a multi-page website. A site with up to 5 pages costs 2,990 PLN net. ## How they differ

**One-page**: all the content sits on one long page. The menu scrolls to each part: services, completed work, reviews, contact.

**Multi-page**: each service has its own page with its own address, e.g. `yourbusiness.pl/bathroom-renovation`.

## When separate pages are better

- a business with several kinds of services or products, for example a [manufacturing company](/en/blog/website-for-a-manufacturing-company) with a few product groups,
- an online shop,
- a business that wants to run a blog or guides,
- a business that wants to show up in Google for many different phrases.

## What it changes in Google

Google shows specific pages in its results, not whole websites. One page usually means one main phrase. Every extra page is another chance.

Example of a renovation company in Rzeszów:

- a **one-page** site has a chance at "renovation company Rzeszów",
- a **multi-page** site can also appear for "bathroom renovation Rzeszów", "tiling Rzeszów" and "flat painting Rzeszów", because each service has its own page.

## Which one sells better

For a single service, a one-page site is often more effective. The customer scrolls from the problem, through your work and the price, down to the phone number, without wandering off.

With several services, separate pages win. A customer coming from Google lands straight on the description of what they're looking for instead of scrolling past everything else.

## A 5-question test

1. Do you have 4 or more separate services? **YES = multi-page**
2. Do you want customers to find you in Google for each service separately? **YES = multi-page**
3. Are you planning a blog or guides? **YES = multi-page**
4. Is your budget small and your time for writing text short? **YES = one-page**
5. Should the site cover one service or one campaign? **YES = one-page**

**Result:** if you answered YES to at least two of questions 1–3, choose multi-page. Otherwise, start with a one-page site.

## FAQ

**Can I start with a one-page site and expand it later?**

You can, but splitting one page into several often means a substantial rebuild: a new layout, new text and redirects from the old addresses. If you know you'll need separate pages within a year, it's cheaper to build them straight away.

**Is a one-page site worse for Google?**

It isn't worse. It simply has a chance at fewer phrases. If you offer one service in one town, a one-page site is perfectly enough.

**What should a one-page site be built with?**

Any technology will do: a site builder, WordPress or a site written from scratch. What matters more is that it loads fast and works well on a phone.
