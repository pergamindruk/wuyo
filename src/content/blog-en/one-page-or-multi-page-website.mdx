It's one of the most common questions from small business owners. There's no single right answer, but there are a few key questions that will help you choose.

## One-page or multi-page: the quick answer for a small business

If you run a small business with 1–3 services and want to launch quickly: **start with a one-page site**. If you have more services, want to rank in Google and plan long-term growth: **choose multi-page from the start**.

## What's the difference between one-page and multi-page?

**One-page website:** all the content on a single HTML page, and the navigation scrolls to sections. The user scrolls instead of clicking through a menu.

**Multi-page website:** each section is a separate page with its own URL. Fuller navigation, and the chance to rank for many phrases in Google.

## Comparison: decision table

| Criterion | One-page | Multi-page |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Build cost | Lower | Higher |
| Build time | Shorter (3–7 days) | Longer (2–4 weeks) |
| SEO potential | Limited (1 URL) | High (many URLs) |
| Conversion | High (linear flow) | Depends on structure |
| Ease of updating | Simple | Needs more work |
| How many services? | 1–3 | 4+ |
| Amount of text | Less | More |

## When to choose a one-page website

<GreenRed
  green="One-page works when: you have one main service or product, want to launch quickly, are running a campaign (landing page), or work locally without needing to rank for many phrases."
  red="One-page won't work when: you have many services needing separate descriptions, want a blog or articles, care about extensive SEO or have an online shop."

**Good uses for one-page:**
- A freelancer's or specialist's business card site
- An ad campaign landing page
- A start-up MVP page
- A simple service business website (1–2 services)
- An artist's or photographer's portfolio

## When to choose a multi-page website

**Good uses for multi-page:**
- A business with several product or service lines
- An online shop
- A blog or content site
- A business wanting to rank organically for many phrases
- A portal or platform with user logins

## Impact on SEO

<Callout type="info">
**The key SEO rule:** Google ranks specific pages (URLs) for specific phrases. One-page has one URL = one SEO goal. Multi-page can have a page for every service, every town, every product, which means many times more organic traffic.
</Callout>

Example: a renovation company

- One-page: ranks for "renovation company Kraków"
- Multi-page: can rank for "wall painting Kraków", "tiling Kraków", "bathroom renovation Kraków", with each phrase a separate page

## Impact on conversion

One-page usually converts better for one specific offer, because it leads the user through a linear sales funnel: problem → solution → price → call to action.

Multi-page gives more freedom, but also more places where the user can get lost and leave without converting. It needs well-planned architecture.

## The 5-question test: what to choose?

Answer YES or NO to each question:

1. Do you have 4 or more separate services or products? → YES = multi-page
2. Is organic search an important way of getting customers? → YES = multi-page
3. Do you want to run a blog or publish articles? → YES = multi-page
4. Are your budget and time limited? → YES = one-page (to start)
5. Is it a campaign site or landing page? → YES = one-page

**Result:** if you answered YES 3+ times to questions 1–3 → multi-page. If YES to 4–5 → one-page.

## FAQ

**Can I start with one-page and expand later?**

Yes. It's a popular strategy: start with one-page (fast, cheap), and when the business grows, expand to multi-page. With well-written code the migration takes a few days, not weeks.

**Is one-page worse for Google?**

It isn't "worse", it's just limited to one keyword. If your business focuses on one service in one town, one-page is completely enough.

**What technology is best for one-page?**

Next.js or plain HTML/CSS are the best options for loading speed. Wix or Squarespace work if you want to build the site yourself without technical knowledge.
