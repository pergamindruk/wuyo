Short answer: a logo from a generator or Canva is enough to test an idea, but for a business meant to run for years it's better to order it from a designer. With me a logo in three versions (horizontal, vertical, mono) costs from 890 PLN net, plus 23% VAT.

More and more often I hear: "I typed my business name into a generator and got a nice logo in a minute. Why should I pay a designer?" A fair question. Below I describe what you get in each case. No scaremongering.

## What you get from a generator or Canva

An AI generator makes a picture. You type in your name and industry and get a few suggestions. Quick and cheap, or free.

Canva gives you ready templates. You choose a design, type in your business name, change the colour. Also quick.

Both work as long as the logo lives on a screen: a Facebook profile, an email footer, the first version of a website. Problems start when the logo has to go to print or onto a sign.

## Where a "free" logo starts costing money

**The file isn't print-ready.** A generator usually gives you a PNG or JPG. It looks fine on screen. On a 3-metre banner or a van wrap the edges get blurry. The print shop asks for a vector file, and you don't have one. Redrawing the logo as a vector is extra work you'll pay for anyway.

**You don't know whether the logo is really yours.** A Canva template may have the same heart, leaf or crown as another business's logo in your industry. With generators you can't be sure what the image was made from either. Some services state in their licence that their elements may not be registered as a trademark. Read the terms before you print 1000 flyers.

**It doesn't work small.** Many generator logos have thin lines, shadows and fine details. On a 90×50 mm business card or a phone icon it turns into a blob.

**No versions for different backgrounds.** You get one version. But you also need a horizontal one for the website, a vertical one for your profile, a single-colour one for a stamp and a white one for dark backgrounds.

## When a generator logo is enough

Not everyone needs a designer from day one. A generator or template is enough when:

<CheckItem>You're testing a business idea and don't know if it'll exist in six months</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>The logo will only be on a social media profile, with no print</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>You're running a one-off event, e.g. a fair or a fundraiser</CheckItem>

In that case don't spend money on design. Spend it on your first customers. Order a logo when the business takes off; how to recognise that moment, I describe in [when to change your company logo](/blog/kiedy-zmienic-logo-firmy).

## When it's better to go to a designer

<CheckItem>You're ordering a shop sign, a banner, a van or window wrap</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>You're printing business cards, flyers, product labels</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>You're making a website and want it to look consistent with everything else</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>You work in an industry where customers compare several businesses and choose the one that looks more serious</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>You're thinking about registering a trademark</CheckItem>

If you ticked even one, the generator logo will need fixing sooner or later. It's cheaper to do it once, at the start.

There's also a middle way. Make a few options in a generator and bring them to our meeting. It's great material for a brief: you can immediately see what style you like, which colours, whether you prefer a symbol or just lettering.

## FAQ

**Is an AI generator logo suitable for print?**

Usually not straight away. A generator gives a PNG or JPG, and large-format print and signs need a vector file. The image has to be redrawn, which is extra work.

**Can I register a Canva logo as a trademark?**

Check the licence of the service you use. Some state that their ready elements and templates may not be registered as a trademark. If in doubt, ask a patent attorney.

**How much does a logo from a designer cost?**

With me from 890 PLN net, plus 23% VAT. The price includes three versions of the logo (horizontal, vertical and single-colour), vector files and full copyright once paid in full.

**Can a designer fix a logo I made in a generator?**

Often, yes. If the idea is good and the only problem is file quality or small details, it can be refined. If the logo resembles another company's mark, it's better to design a new one.
