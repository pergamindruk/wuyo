A guesthouse website has one job: a guest who found you on a booking portal should book directly with you next time.

This is for owners of guesthouses, farm stays and holiday cottages. The rooms are well kept, the reviews are good, and every portal booking still costs you a commission. On top of that, in high season the phone rings from morning to night with the same questions.

## The portal brings guests, your website brings them back

Booking portals are good for a start. They have traffic you won't build on your own. The catch is that a guest from a portal belongs to the portal: next trip, they go back there and compare you with twenty other places.

Your own website won't replace that overnight. But it does three things a portal won't:

1. It takes bookings from guests who come back or were recommended to you.
2. It shows your place the way you want: with the area around it, the story of the house, the house rules.
3. It can show up in Google for searches like “rooms with a mountain view” or “farm stay for families Podkarpacie”.

## A website for a guesthouse: what it needs

Every point on this list is a question the guest will ask anyway. Either on the phone or in a message through the portal.

<CheckItem>Photos of each room separately: beds, bathroom, view from the window. Taken during the day, in window light</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>How many people the room sleeps, what beds it has, whether a cot for a child fits</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Prices written out: high season, low season, long weekends, minimum number of nights</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>What's included: breakfast, parking, towels, barbecue, sauna</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>House rules: pets, check-in and check-out times, quiet hours</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Deposit: how much, by when, what happens if the guest cancels</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Directions and a map. Can an ordinary car get there in winter, where to park</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>The area: trails, a lake, things to do with kids, distances in minutes</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Guest reviews, ideally from your Google Business Profile</CheckItem>

Photos and prices do the most work. Guests choose with their eyes, then they do the maths. If they have to write in to find out the August price, they often don't.

## Bookings on your own website: three ways

Not every guesthouse needs a booking system with a calendar from day one. You have three options.

**An enquiry form.** The guest picks dates, the number of people and a room, and leaves a name, phone number and email. The request comes to you, you confirm it and send the bank details for the deposit. Simple, and enough to start with.

**A button to the system you already use.** If you run your calendar in booking software, I put a clear button on the site that takes guests straight to it. Nothing gets duplicated.

**Booking with online payment.** The guest sees free dates and pays the deposit right away through PayU, Przelewy24, Tpay or Stripe. Convenient, but it's a bigger website and I price it after we talk.

<Callout type="info">
Before you add a calendar to your website, make sure it syncs with the portals. Two bookings for the same room on a long weekend is the worst possible start.
</Callout>

## One page or several

A small place with three rooms and one cottage fits easily on a single page. Photos, prices, rules, a map, a form.

A bigger guesthouse with different room types, a hall for events or group offers sells better across several pages. Rooms on one, family events on another, the area on a third. Each page can then catch different Google searches. I wrote more about this choice in [one-page or multi-page website](/en/blog/one-page-or-multi-page-website).

## Your Google profile sends guests to your website

Someone who types “accommodation” with the name of a town first sees a map with places to stay. Reviews, photos and a complete profile win there. A link to your website from the profile is the easiest way for a guest to skip the portal.

Make sure the prices, check-in times and rules are the same on your profile, your website and the portals. A guest who finds three different prices doesn't know which one to believe. How to set up the profile is covered in [Google Business Profile for a small business](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business).

## FAQ

**How much does a guesthouse website cost?**

With me, a one-page site is from 1,990 PLN net and a business site with up to 5 pages is 2,990 PLN net. Add 23% VAT. The domain and hosting for the first year are included.

**Should I drop the booking portals once I have a website?**

No. Portals still bring in new guests. The website is there to take the ones who come back, were recommended to you or found you on Google.

**Should I show room prices on the website?**

Yes. Give high and low season prices and the minimum number of nights. If the price depends on the dates, write “from” and say what it depends on.

**What should I prepare before ordering a website?**

Photos of the rooms and the area, your price list, house and deposit rules, directions and a link to your Google profile. The full list is in [what to prepare for a website](/en/blog/what-to-prepare-for-a-website).
