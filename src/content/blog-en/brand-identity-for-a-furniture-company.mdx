## How a furniture company differs from other businesses

Most businesses show their logo on paper and screens. Yours also ends up on wood, plywood, metal and fabric. It gets burned, engraved, milled or sewn on, and that changes how it has to be designed.

<GreenRed
  green="A good logo for a joiner: a simple shape, clear lettering and a one-colour version. You can engrave it on a small plate, cut it from plywood and print it on an invoice."
  red="A weak logo for a joiner: thin lines, a fine wood-grain drawing, shading and three colours. It looks good on screen, but burned into oak it turns into a dark blot."

So before you approve a design, ask to see it at a small size. If the mark is unreadable at 3 cm wide, it won't work on a plate by the hinge or on a drawer.

Engraving and milling need a vector file, which can be enlarged without losing quality. I listed the formats you should receive in [which logo files to get from your designer](/en/blog/logo-files-from-designer).

## What a furniture company's identity should include

You don't need a thick manual and twenty templates. Stick to the things a client will actually see or pick up:

<CheckItem>A logo in horizontal, vertical and one-colour versions</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Colours and typefaces written down in a short brand book</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Business cards to leave behind after measuring up</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A label or plate for finished furniture, with your name and phone number</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A catalogue or quote template with photos of your work</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A sign for the workshop or showroom</CheckItem>

The cheapest advertising here is a label on the furniture. Stick it inside a door or on the side of a drawer. It doesn't spoil the look, and your number stays with the client for years. When they want a matching chest of drawers five years later, they won't have to go looking for you.

Remember that your logo will often sit on wood. Yellow and light beige disappear on pine, and dark brown disappears on walnut. I showed how to pick colours that work on any background in [how to choose logo colours](/en/blog/how-to-choose-logo-colours).

**How much does a logo for a joiner cost?**

**Does a small joinery shop need a brand book?**

Not a full one. A short one is enough to keep the colours the same on signs, T-shirts and labels from different suppliers. A mini brand book for an existing logo costs 590 PLN net.

**Can I keep my old logo?**

Yes, if people already recognise it. A facelift is usually enough: I simplify and fix the mark without changing its character. It costs from 1,000 PLN net.
