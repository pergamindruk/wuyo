Short answer: a shop on a ready-made subscription platform costs 0–3,000 PLN to start and 100–500 PLN a month. A shop built for you starts at about 6,900 PLN. The difference isn't the look, but who owns the shop and how far you can grow it.

Below is a breakdown of the three routes, the hidden costs and a hint on which to choose at what scale. Prices in PLN, net, for the Polish market.

## Three routes and their real costs

| Solution | Start-up cost | Monthly cost | For whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subscription platform | 0–3,000 PLN | 100–500 PLN | Starting out, testing an idea, up to 100 products |
| WooCommerce | 3,000–12,000 PLN | 100–300 PLN | Medium catalogue, need for plugins |
| Custom-built shop | from 6,900 PLN | 50–200 PLN | Own brand, unusual way of selling |

Watch the monthly amounts: they hurt the most, because they come back every month for years. A shop at 500 PLN a month is 30,000 PLN over five years. That's more than many shops built from scratch.

## Subscription platform

You pay a subscription, get a ready shop, choose a template and add your products.

**In its favour:**

- You launch in a few days, without a contractor
- Payments, shipping and courier integrations work straight away
- Someone else looks after updates and security
- Low entry cost, so a cheap way to test an idea

**What to expect along the way:**

- The subscription grows with turnover and the number of features
- The look is limited by the template; everyone looks similar
- Some platforms take a commission on sales
- Moving elsewhere can be painful, as your data and template stay in the system

It's a sensible choice if **you don't know yet whether your product will sell**. There's no point ordering a 10,000 PLN shop to test a hypothesis.

## WooCommerce

A shop built on WordPress with a shop plugin. The most popular solution in Poland.

**In its favour:**

- There's a plugin for anything you can think of
- It's easy to find a contractor, since lots of companies work with it
- No commission on sales

**What to expect:**

- Premium plugins have their own subscriptions and add up faster than you think
- Updates have to be done regularly, otherwise the shop becomes a target
- With a large catalogue it can slow down and need more server power
- A plugin that hasn't been developed for two years can bring the shop down after one update

A good choice for a medium catalogue and typical selling. Make sure the contractor calculates the cost of plugins **per year**, not just at launch.

## A custom-built shop

A shop written from scratch, usually on a modern front-end engine, with a content management system and connected payments.

**In its favour:**

- Very fast, which helps sales and search rankings
- The look and checkout process fit your product, not a template
- No plugins to maintain and a smaller attack surface
- Low monthly costs
- The shop is yours, including the code

**What to expect:**

- A higher entry cost
- A longer build
- Every new feature is developer work, not installing a plugin

## Costs nobody mentions in the quote

This is the most common source of disappointment. Building the shop itself is usually 60–70% of first-year spending.

| Item | Approximate cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | 50–150 PLN / year | Registered to you, not the contractor |
| Hosting | 300–1,500 PLN / year | Depends on traffic and catalogue size |
| Product photos | 20–100 PLN / product | The biggest item with a large catalogue |
| Product descriptions | 30–150 PLN / product | Copying the manufacturer's text hurts rankings |
| Payment integration | 0–1,000 PLN | Plus a commission on every transaction |
| Courier integration | 0–2,000 PLN | Depends on the number of carriers |
| Care and updates | 150–700 PLN / month | With WooCommerce it's not optional, it's necessary |
| Terms and privacy policy | 300–1,500 PLN | A lawyer does this, not a designer |

<Callout type="warning">
**Product photos and descriptions are the most often underestimated item.** With a catalogue of 200 products this can cost more than the shop itself. Before you order a shop, count how many products you have and who will prepare materials for them.
</Callout>

## What affects the build price

- **Number of products and variants:** sizes, colours and versions multiply the work
- **How shipping is calculated:** a flat rate is simple, weight and size based isn't
- **Integrations:** warehouse, accounting, wholesaler, courier system
- **Customer accounts and order history:** an extra module, not standard
- **Multiple languages and currencies:** can raise the quote by half
- **Selling on other channels:** marketplaces are a separate integration
- **Migration from an existing shop:** moving products, orders and addresses

## How to choose on a limited budget

The order that saves the most money:

1. **Check whether the product sells:** a subscription platform, or selling through social media and classifieds sites
2. **Work out what one order costs you:** only then do you know what shop you can afford to run
3. **Build your own shop once sales are repeatable:** then every second of speed and every simplified checkout step pays back in cash

The reverse order, shop first and sales later, usually ends with an abandoned shop and a grudge against the contractor.

## FAQ

**How much does an online shop cost?**

A ready subscription platform is 0–3,000 PLN to start and 100–500 PLN a month. WooCommerce is usually 3,000–12,000 PLN to build. A custom-built shop starts at 6,900 PLN with me. With every option you need to add photos, product descriptions, hosting and care.

**What's cheaper: WooCommerce or a subscription platform?**

In the first year usually the subscription platform. Over three or more years most often WooCommerce or your own shop, because you don't pay a growing subscription or sales commission. The break-even point shifts with turnover.

**How much does running an online shop cost per month?**

From about 50–200 PLN for your own shop, through 100–300 PLN for WooCommerce with care, to 100–500 PLN for subscription platforms. On top of that comes the payment operator's commission on every transaction.

**Do I need a shop to sell online?**

Not at the start. Many businesses begin by selling through social media, classifieds sites or a simple order form on their website. A shop makes sense when there are so many orders that handling them by hand costs you more than running a shop.

**How long does it take to build an online shop?**

A subscription platform: a few days. WooCommerce: 3–6 weeks. A custom-built shop: with me 30–45 working days, depending on the number of products and integrations. What usually takes the longest isn't the technical work, but the client's product photos and descriptions.

**How much does an online shop with 500 products cost?**

The number of products rarely changes the build price; it changes the amount of work entering them. Typing in 500 products by hand takes dozens of hours; importing from a wholesaler's file or a previous shop takes much less. On a subscription platform check the product limit of your plan too, as the subscription usually rises with a larger catalogue.

**Is a subscription online shop worth it?**

At the start, yes: you pay little and quickly find out whether the product sells. After a few years, not necessarily: a 500 PLN monthly subscription is 30,000 PLN over five years, more than many shops built from scratch. Work out the cost over three years, not the first month.

**Who should prepare product photos and descriptions?**

You can do it yourself or outsource it, but someone has to, and it's worth costing it before ordering the shop. Copying the manufacturer's descriptions is the worst option: identical text in many shops weakens search visibility.
