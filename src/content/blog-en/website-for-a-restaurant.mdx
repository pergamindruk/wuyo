A restaurant website has to answer three questions for a hungry person holding a phone: what do you serve, are you open right now, and how do I get there. If they can't find one of those answers in a few seconds, they go back to the search results and pick the place next door.

This post is for owners of restaurants, pizzerias, bistros, home-cooking lunch bars and cafés. Places where the food is good, but in Google the winner is whoever shows their menu better.

## Who looks for a restaurant online

Usually someone who's hungry now or planning an evening out. They type "pizza Rzeszów", "home-cooked lunch Rzeszów" or "restaurant for a birthday Rzeszów". They look at the map, open two or three places and compare.

The nicest photo of the dining room doesn't win that comparison. The place that wins is the one where the menu with prices, the opening hours and a phone number or booking button are visible straight away.

## A website for a restaurant: what it must include

I start every hospitality website with this list. Each point is a question guests will ask anyway, by phone or in a message.

<CheckItem>The menu with prices as text, not a PDF or a photo of the printed menu. It reads on a phone without zooming</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Opening hours visible right away, including when the kitchen closes if that's earlier than the venue</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Address, map and a note on how to get there, for example where to park</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A phone number that dials with one tap</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Table booking, or takeaway and delivery ordering if you offer them</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Photos of real dishes and the interior, taken in daylight</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Allergens next to dishes, marked with simple symbols</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Information about private parties and events, if you host them</CheckItem>

The menu is the foundation. Google reads text, not a photo of a menu card. When someone searches for "pierogi Rzeszów" and pierogi are on the menu on your site, you have a chance to show up. If the menu only exists as a photo, Google can't read the dish. It handles PDFs worse than an ordinary web page.

## The daily menu: who updates it and how fast

This is the first thing I ask every restaurant owner. The answer decides how I build the site.

If you have a fixed menu that changes twice a year, you can leave the updates to me. You send the new menu and I put it up.

If you have a lunch set that changes every day or every week, you need a panel where you can enter it yourself. In that case I build the site on WordPress. In the morning you type in the soup and the main course, click "Save" and you're done. I show you how in a one-hour training session that I record, so you can go back to it. The difference between a WordPress site and one built from scratch is covered in [WordPress or a custom website](/en/blog/wordpress-or-custom-website).

<Callout type="info">
You can keep posting the daily menu on Facebook, like you do now. The website then only needs the fixed menu and a note on where to find the daily set. Don't change your guests' habits just because you're getting a website.
</Callout>

## Table booking and takeaway orders

Not every restaurant needs a booking system. A lunch bar lives off people walking in from the street. A restaurant that runs out of tables on Friday nights does need one.

To start with, a simple form is often enough: day, time, number of people, phone number and notes, for example a high chair for a child. The request lands in your inbox and you or a waiter confirm by phone.

If you already use a delivery app or a booking system, we don't rebuild anything. I put a clear button on the site that takes guests to your ordering page. Nobody has to hunt for it.

## The website, Google Maps and Facebook work together

When someone searches for "restaurant Rzeszów", they first see a map with a few places and the websites below it. On the map, the places with reviews, photos and up-to-date hours win. The Google Business Profile then sends people to the website with the full menu.

It works best when all three say the same thing: a Google Business Profile with reviews and photos, a website with the menu and booking, and Facebook or Instagram with the latest dishes. Opening hours must match everywhere, holidays included. How to set up the profile is covered in [Google Business Profile for a small business](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business).

## FAQ

**How much does a restaurant website cost?**

With me, a one-page website starts at 1,990 PLN net and a business website with up to 5 pages is 2,990 PLN net. Add 23% VAT. The domain and hosting for the first year are included.

**Is a PDF menu enough?**

As an extra to download, yes. As the only menu, no. On a phone you have to zoom into a PDF, and Google doesn't see the dishes in it as well as in plain text.

**I have a Facebook page. Why do I need a website?**

Facebook is good for the daily menu and news. A website gets you into Google results for specific dishes and gives you one place with the fixed menu, prices and directions. More in [website or Facebook page](/en/blog/website-or-facebook-page).

**What should I prepare before ordering a website?**

Your current menu with prices, opening hours, a dozen or so photos of dishes and the interior, a few sentences about the place and a link to your Google Business Profile. The full list is in [what to prepare for a website](/en/blog/what-to-prepare-for-a-website).
