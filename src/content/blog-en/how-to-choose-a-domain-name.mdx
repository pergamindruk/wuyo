How do you choose a domain for your business? In short: short, easy to dictate over the phone, ending in .pl if you work in Poland, and registered to you. And if I build your website (from 1,990 PLN net, plus 23% VAT), the domain for the first year is included.

A domain is your website's address, e.g. `kowalskibakery.pl`. You choose it once, and then it lives on your business cards, your van, your email signature and in Google. Changing it years later hurts, so it's worth spending 15 minutes on it now.

## How to choose a domain for your business: 7 rules

### 1. Short and no spelling out

The test is simple. Dictate the address to someone over the phone. If you have to add "with an h" or "with a hyphen in the middle", the address is too hard. The shorter it is, the fewer mistakes.

### 2. The .pl ending

If you work in Poland, choose .pl. People type it out of habit. Endings like .com.pl, .eu or .net aren't bad, but customers will often type .pl anyway and end up somewhere else. If the .pl is taken, it's better to change the name than the ending.

### 3. No Polish characters

A domain with "ą" or "ł" can be registered, but it causes trouble. Some customers will type it without the accents, and email addresses with Polish characters may not get delivered. Instead of `złotarączka.pl` choose `zlotaraczka.pl`.

### 4. A hyphen only as a last resort

`auto-serwis-nowak.pl` looks worse than `autoserwisnowak.pl`, and the hyphen is easy to lose when dictating. One hyphen is fine when without it the words run together into something unreadable. Two is too many.

### 5. Business name or service plus town

This is the question I hear most. You have two options:

- **business name**, e.g. `kowalskibudownictwo.pl`: suits you if people know you by name and you plan to grow,
- **service plus town**, e.g. `hydraulikrzeszow.pl`: says straight away what you do, but ties you to one town and one service.

The word "Rzeszów" in your domain won't get you to first place in Google. Good website content and a [Google Business Profile](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business) do much more. That's why I usually recommend the business name.

### 6. Check that the name is free and clean

Before you buy, type the name into Google. See whether there's already a business with a similar name in your industry. Also check whether anyone has a registered trademark. The worst scenario is a website, business cards and a wrapped van, and a letter from a lawyer a year later.

You can check whether a domain is free in the search box of any registrar. Just type in the name.

### 7. Register it to yourself

This is the rule you can't skip. The domain must be registered to your business, not to the designer, the IT guy or a friend's son. If it's in someone else's name, when you part ways you can lose your website address, your position in Google and all the email addresses on that domain. ## Domain and email address

A domain isn't just your website. It's also your email address. `office@kowalskibudownictwo.pl` looks more professional than `kowalski.budowa123@gmail.com`, especially on an offer or invoice.

So once more: short and no Polish characters. You'll be dictating this address over the phone several times a week.

## FAQ

**Is .pl or .com.pl better?**

For a business working in Poland, .pl. Customers type it out of habit. Choose .com.pl only when the .pl is taken and you don't want to change the name.

**Does a town name in the domain help with Google?**

A little, but less than you'd think. Your position in local results depends much more on website content and your Google Business Profile. A town in the domain makes sense when the business name is long or difficult.

**My domain is registered to someone else. What should I do?**

Ask that person to transfer the domain to your business. The registrar has a procedure for it. Do it now, while the relationship is good, not when you part ways.

**Can I change my domain once the website is live?**

You can, but the old domain should redirect to the new one for a long time. Otherwise you'll lose traffic from Google and people who have your old address on a business card.
