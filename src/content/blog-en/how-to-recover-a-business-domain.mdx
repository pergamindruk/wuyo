## Domain, server and website are three different things

Before you start chasing anyone, separate three things. Each one is recovered differently.

- **The domain** is the address, for example `yourbusiness.pl`. You renew it every year with a company that registers domains.
- **The server** (hosting) is where the website files live. You pay for it separately, sometimes with the same company as the domain.
- **The website** is the files and the content. It can be rebuilt or made from scratch.

The domain matters most. A website can be made again, but the address your customers know, your email addresses and your Google position are all tied to the domain.

## Check who the domain is registered to

For `.pl` domains the owner is called the registrant (abonent in Polish). The registrant decides about the domain, not whoever pays for it.

<CheckItem>Type your domain into the WHOIS search at dns.pl. You'll see which company the domain is registered with and when it's paid until</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>If the registrant is a company, its name is usually visible. A private person's details are hidden</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Go through old emails and invoices. Look for the registrar's name and the words “domain renewal”</CheckItem>

That leaves two situations: the domain is in your company's name, or in the contractor's.

## When the domain is in your name

This is the easier case. The domain is yours, you're just missing access to the account it sits on.

1. Write to the registrar from the email address given at registration, or with a company document. Ask for access to the account or for the transfer code, called the AuthInfo code.
2. Open an account with a registrar of your choice and move the domain there with that code.
3. Set the password and contact details to yourself, not to the contractor or an employee.

The registrar deals with the registrant. If the domain is in your name, you don't need the previous contractor's consent.

## When the domain is in the contractor's name

Then the domain formally belongs to them. To take it over you need a change of registrant, called an assignment. It's done with the registrar and both sides have to agree.

## Server and website files

The server works much like the domain. If the account is in your name, you'll get access back from the hosting company. If it's in the contractor's name, ask them for a copy of the files and the database.

If the files can't be recovered, that's not the end of the world. I can copy the text and photos from what's visible in the browser and build the site again. ## Watch out for email on your domain

The most common slip when moving: the domain goes to a new company, someone changes the settings, and the `office@yourbusiness.pl` mailboxes stop receiving mail. Customers write, and the messages bounce or get lost.

<CheckItem>Before you move anything, note where your email runs: with the same provider as the website or separately</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Back up important emails from the mailbox</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>After the change, send yourself a message from a private address and check it arrived</CheckItem>
<CheckItem ok={false}>Moving the domain on a Friday evening without checking email</CheckItem>

## FAQ

**Do I need the previous contractor's consent to move the domain?**

No, if your company is the domain's registrant. Then you ask the registrar for the transfer code. You need the contractor's consent when the domain is registered to them, because a change of registrant requires both sides to agree.

**How do I check who a .pl domain is registered to?**

Type the domain into the WHOIS search at dns.pl. You'll see the registrar and the expiry date. A company name is usually visible, a private person's details are hidden.

**Will I lose my Google position after moving the domain?**

No, if the address stays the same and the website keeps working without a break. Problems start when the site disappears for a long time or the address changes without redirects.

**What if there are no files from the old website?**

Build the website again. The text and photos can be copied from what's visible in the browser. What matters is keeping the domain, because that's what your customers and email are tied to.
