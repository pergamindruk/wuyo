The copyright to your logo should pass to you as part of the design price. With me a logo costs from 890 PLN net (plus 23% VAT), and once it's paid in full you get the full economic rights and the source files.

Sounds simple. In practice, many business owners find out their logo isn't theirs only when they want to register it, switch designers or print it on a shop sign. Below I explain how to check this before you pay. I describe Polish law, which applies to my contracts.

## Who owns the copyright right after the design is made

The author of the logo is the person who drew it. If the design is creative, and not, say, just a name typed in a standard font, copyright protects it from the moment it's created, and the rights belong to the author, not to you. Even if you've paid the invoice.

For the economic rights to pass to you, you need a contract. Under the Polish Copyright Act, a transfer of economic rights must be made in writing, otherwise it's void. An email saying "sure, the logo is yours" isn't enough.

## Transfer of rights or a licence: what's the difference for you

These are two different things, and it's worth telling them apart before you sign.

A licence makes sense for stock photos or fonts. For a logo that should serve you for years, you want a transfer of rights.

## What a logo copyright transfer contract must contain

The contract doesn't need ten pages. It needs a few specific clauses.

<CheckItem>A clear statement that the **economic copyright is transferred**, not that a licence is granted</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A list of **fields of use**: print, internet, outdoor advertising, marking goods and services, registration as a trademark. The law requires them to be listed. Whatever isn't in the contract, you don't get</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Consent to **changes and adaptations**, so that another designer can refresh your logo one day</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>The moment the rights pass to you, usually once paid in full</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A list of the files you get: vectors (SVG, PDF, EPS) and PNG</CheckItem>

Fields of use are the most common gap in cheap contracts. If the contract only says "print and internet", and in three years you want vehicle graphics or embroidered T-shirts, you may formally need the author's consent.

## What can't be transferred

The author's moral rights. That's the designer's right to be recognised as the author of the design. They can't be sold or waived.

In practice this doesn't get in your way. The designer can't stop you using the logo if the economic rights have been transferred to you. Showing the design in a portfolio is usually agreed in the contract. Mine includes such a clause, and if you don't want the design in my portfolio, we strike it out at the start.

## A logo from Canva, a generator or "for 100 PLN"

Here the rights problem is different. A designer can't transfer rights to something that isn't theirs. A Canva template or an element from a paid stock library is covered by that service's licence. Some services forbid registering such elements as a trademark in their terms.

## What to do when your former designer won't hand over the files

First, find the contract and invoice. Check what they say about rights.

- **You have a contract with a transfer of rights:** ask in writing for the source files. You have grounds for it.
- **You have no contract:** ask the designer to sign a transfer contract now. Usually it's one page and a bit of goodwill.
- **The designer doesn't reply or refuses:** consider whether the logo is worth the fight. Sometimes a refresh or a new mark costs less than a dispute. If the mark is key to your business, talk to a lawyer or a patent attorney.

When refreshing a logo makes sense and when it's better to start over is covered in [when to change your logo](/en/blog/when-to-change-your-logo).

## FAQ

**If I pay for a logo, do I automatically get the copyright?**

No. Paying for the design doesn't transfer the rights. You need a written contract that lists the fields of use.

**Does transferring logo copyright cost extra?**

It depends on the designer. Some charge for it separately. With me the economic rights are included in the logo price, from 890 PLN net plus 23% VAT, and pass to you once it's paid in full.

**Is logo copyright the same as a trademark?**

No. Copyright protects the graphic design itself and arises together with it. A trademark is registered with the Polish Patent Office (or the EUIPO for the whole EU), for specific industries and for a separate fee. The office doesn't check copyright when you file, but a trademark filed in breach of someone else's rights can later be invalidated. So first the transfer contract, then the filing.

**Can the designer show my logo in their portfolio?**

If the contract allows it. After a transfer of economic rights, showing the design in a portfolio is best agreed in writing. It doesn't take away your rights to the logo. If discretion matters to you, put it in the contract.
