## Who looks for a garage on Google

Usually someone whose car has a problem. A warning light came on, something knocks when they brake, the tyres need changing before winter. They're in a car park or at work, phone in hand, opening a few results one after another.

That person wants four answers, fast:

1. Do they fix my problem, and my make of car?
2. Where are they, and can I get there?
3. Are they open today?
4. How soon can I book in?

## A website for a car repair shop: what has to be on it

I start every garage site from this list. Each item answers a question the customer would ask on the phone anyway.

<CheckItem>A list of services. Be specific: “timing belt replacement”, “air conditioning service”, “computer diagnostics”, “tyre change and storage”, not “automotive services”</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>The makes and types of car you work on. If you don't do vans or electric cars, say so too</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Address, map and a directions tip, for example “entrance next to the petrol station”</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Opening hours, including Saturdays and before holidays</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A phone number at the very top that can be tapped on a phone</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Photos of the workshop and bays. Real ones, taken on your phone</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Customer reviews, ideally pulled from your Google Business Profile</CheckItem>

Hours and phone number are the basics. Someone with a broken car won't hunt for your number in the footer. If they don't see it straight away, they go back to the results and call someone else.

## A separate page for each service

People don't search for “car repair shop”. They search for “tyre change Rzeszów”, “car air conditioning Łańcut”, “Audi mechanic Rzeszów”. Google shows the page that answers that exact question.

That's why I usually recommend a site with several pages to garages that offer a few services. Mechanics on one page, tyres on another, air conditioning on a third. Each one gets a few sentences: what you do, how long it takes, whether people need to book. I explain the difference between one page and several in [one-page or multi-page website](/en/blog/one-page-or-multi-page-website).

If you only do one thing, say tyres, a one-page site is enough.

## Online booking instead of answering the phone mid-job

Your hands are covered in oil and the phone rings for the fifth time this hour. Everyone asks the same thing: “when can I come in for a tyre change?”.

A simple booking form takes the load off the phone. The customer fills in:

- make, model and year of the car,
- what needs doing (picked from your list of services),
- registration number or VIN, if parts are involved,
- a day that suits them,
- their phone number.

The request lands in your inbox. You call back when you have a minute, and you already know which parts to order.

<Callout type="info">
The form doesn't have to book a time slot in a calendar straight away. To start with, a request you confirm by phone is enough. A calendar with free slots can be added later.
</Callout>

## The website and your Google profile work together

When someone types “mechanic Rzeszów”, Google first shows a map with three garages. The websites come below it. A garage without a Google Business Profile loses those customers before they ever see its website.

They work best together: a profile with reviews, photos and hours, linking to a website with the full list of services and the booking form. The hours must match in both places. ## FAQ

**How much does a website for a car repair shop cost?**

With me, a one-page site is from 1,990 PLN net and a business site with up to 5 pages is 2,990 PLN net. Add 23% VAT. The domain and hosting for the first year are included.

**Should I put service prices on my garage website?**

For simple jobs, yes. Tyre changes, air conditioning service and diagnostics usually have a fixed price, and customers want to know it before they drive over. For repairs you can't give an exact price, so just say the quote comes after diagnosis.

**I have a Facebook page. Isn't that enough?**

Facebook helps, but it doesn't show up on Google for “mechanic” plus a town name the way a website with separate service pages does. More on that in [website or Facebook page](/en/blog/website-or-facebook-page).

**What should I prepare before ordering a website?**

Your list of services, car makes, opening hours, a few phone photos of the workshop and a link to your Google profile. The full list is in [what to prepare for a website](/en/blog/what-to-prepare-for-a-website).
