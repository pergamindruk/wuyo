Three approaches, their pros and cons, what they cost, and how to choose the one that brings you a real return on investment. Prices in PLN, for the Polish market.

## Four main routes to your website

- **Site builder (Wix, Tilda):** the fastest and cheapest, minimal technical knowledge
- **WordPress:** a balance of flexibility and ease, needs hosting
- **Next.js / code:** full control, high conversion, needs investment
- **Mixed:** a site builder as an MVP, then expanding in WordPress or code

## Option 1: Site builders (Wix, Tilda, Webflow)

Site builders are ready "drag and drop" tools. Ideal for business owners who want a website **tomorrow**, not in a month.

  green="Pros: live in 1–3 days. Cost 150–500 PLN/month. No coding. SSL, domain and hosting built in. Technical support included."
  red="Cons: the site isn't yours; the provider's terms can change and the account can be blocked. Weaker SEO than a site written from scratch. Slower than a coded site. Little flexibility."

**Who for?** Start-ups on a micro budget, an artist's portfolio, a business card site for a local business, a campaign landing page.

## Option 2: WordPress (with templates or custom)

WordPress powers roughly forty percent of websites on the internet. It's a content management system (CMS) that gives you full control but needs hosting.

<GreenRed
  green="Pros: flexible, you can do whatever you want. SEO-friendly (Yoast, Rank Math). A huge ecosystem (60k+ plugins). Your data on your own server."
  red="Cons: hosting is separate (~40–150 PLN/month). It needs maintaining (updates, plugins). Security risk (a careless plugin = an attack). Slower than code written from scratch."
/>

**Who for?** Blogs, e-commerce (WooCommerce), SEO agencies, businesses that want a blog plus a landing page.

## Option 3: Next.js / code written from scratch

This is it. Your own code, full control, high performance. It needs investment, but the return is big.

<GreenRed
  green="Pros: very fast (1–2s vs 5–7s for competitors). Premium SEO: full control over HTML, JSON-LD and metadata. Unlimited design. Unlimited scalability. Security."
  red="Cons: cost 2,900–20,000 PLN+. Build time 2–8 weeks. You need a developer you can call. Hosting can cost more for high performance."
/>

**When Next.js makes sense:**
- You plan to scale (you want to grow organically)
- Conversion is your #1 metric (every 1% means real money)
- A very competitive industry (you need to stand out)
- You have a budget over 2,500 PLN to start
- You want a website that will work for 5+ years

## How to choose a contractor

Whichever option you choose, the contractor matters. Here are 6 questions to ask:

**1. What's your process? (brief → design → code → launch)**

A good contractor has a clear process. If they don't know what they're doing step by step, run.

**2. Will I have access to the code / admin panel?**

Yes. Always. If they say "no", it isn't your website, it's theirs.

**3. What's included in the price? What's extra?**

No "surprise" costs. The website should have a fixed price or a clearly defined scope.

**4. Do I get support after launch? For how long?**

It may be paid, but there should be, say, 2–4 weeks of free support and bug fixes.

**5. Do you build websites for my industry?**

The portfolio is everything. Check case studies from your industry or a similar one.

**6. What's your experience with SEO and conversion?**

A "beautiful website" isn't enough. It also has to be found, and it should convert.

<Callout type="warning">
**Red flags: run from this contractor:**
- No portfolio or few case studies
- Promises "#1 in Google in 3 months"
- No clear timeline, "it's done when it's done"
- Poor communication, no reply within 24h
- Doesn't ask about your business and wants to start straight away
- Won't sign a contract
</Callout>

## 5 steps to your website

1. **Brief:** the contractor asks about your business, competition and goals. You provide text, photos and logo.
2. **Proposal:** they show the plan: site structure, timeline, price, scope.
3. **Design:** a mock-up (how the site will look) to approve. Revisions up to 2–3 rounds.
4. **Development:** building on a test server. Testing, SEO, performance, mobile.
5. **Launch and support:** publishing the site, support in the first month.

## Summary: which option to choose?

**I choose Wix/Tilda if:** I'm on a micro budget (< 1,000 PLN), need a site over the weekend, don't want to code, and it's a business card site (not a lead generator). I'll switch to WordPress or Next.js in a year when I grow.

**I choose WordPress if:** I want a blog and a sales page, have my own developer or will maintain it ourselves. A budget of 1–2k for the build. I need flexibility, but without huge costs.

**I choose Next.js if:** it's the main website of the business. Conversion matters. I'm planning to scale. I have a budget of 3–8k PLN and I'm ready for ongoing support. ## FAQ

**How long does it take to create a business website?**

Site builder (Wix, Tilda): 1–3 days. WordPress with templates: 1–2 weeks. A site written from scratch (Next.js): 2–4 weeks.

**Which option should a small business choose?**

For a small business on a small budget: a site builder. If you want more features: WordPress. If you're planning to scale and want high conversion: a site written from scratch.

**Is WordPress better than Wix?**

There's no "better". WordPress gives more control but needs maintenance. Wix is easier but less flexible. The choice depends on your needs.

**Do I need a domain and hosting on top of building the website?**

Yes. A domain (~yourname.pl): about 40–50 PLN a year. Hosting depends on the platform: Wix and Tilda include hosting, WordPress needs separate hosting (~30–200 PLN a month). With my websites the domain and server for the first year are included.
