## Who looks for a salon on Google

Usually someone who already knows what they want. They type "brow tint Rzeszów", "gel manicure Rzeszów" or "hydrogen facial Rzeszów", open a few results and compare.

That person wants four answers, fast:

1. Do you offer this treatment?
2. How much is it?
3. What does it look like afterwards?
4. When can I come in and how do I book?

## A website for a beauty salon: what it has to include

I start every salon site from this list. Each point answers a question the client would otherwise send you in a message.

<CheckItem>A list of treatments with a short description: what it involves, how long it takes, who it's for</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>The price list as text, not a PDF or a photo, so it reads on a phone without zooming</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Photos of results and of the salon. Real ones, taken on a phone in good light</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A booking button at the very top that works with one tap on a phone</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Address, map and a short note on how to get there, for example “entrance from the courtyard, parking in front of the building”</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Opening hours, Saturdays included</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A few sentences about you: training, courses, how long you've been doing this</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Client reviews, ideally pulled from your Google Business Profile</CheckItem>

The price list matters most. Most messages to a salon start with "how much". If the answer is on the site, the client only writes to book a time.

One rule for before and after photos: show clients' faces and hands only with their consent. The easiest way is to ask during the visit and note it on the client card.

## A separate page for each type of treatment

People search for a specific treatment plus a town. Google shows the page that answers that exact question, not a general "beauty salon" page.

That's why I usually suggest a site with several pages for salons that offer more than one kind of service. One for face, one for nails, one for lashes and brows. Each with the treatments, prices, photos of results and answers to common questions, like how to prepare and how long the effect lasts. The difference between one page and several is explained in [one-page or multi-page website](/en/blog/one-page-or-multi-page-website).

If you only do one thing, say nails, a one-page site is enough.

## Online booking: Booksy or a form on the site

You have a client in the chair and your phone buzzes every fifteen minutes. Every message asks about a free slot.

If you already take bookings through Booksy or another booking system, there's nothing to rebuild. I put a clear "Book a visit" button on the site that takes clients straight to your calendar. They see the free times and book themselves.

If you don't use any system, a simple form does the job. The client fills in:

- which treatment they want (picked from a list),
- a day and time that suits them,
- a phone number,
- notes, for example allergies or that it's their first visit.

The request lands in your inbox and you confirm the time when you have a break.

<Callout type="info">
To start, a form or a button to the system you already use is enough. Don't change how you take bookings just because you're getting a website.
</Callout>

## Instagram, Google Business Profile and the website work together

Instagram shows your work and builds trust. But someone who searches "lash extensions Rzeszów" first sees a map with three salons, and the websites below it. A salon with only Instagram, no Google profile and no website, shows up in neither.

They work best together: a Google Business Profile with reviews and photos, a link from it to the website with prices and booking, and a link from the website to Instagram for your latest work. Hours and prices must match in every one of these places. How to set up the profile is covered in [Google Business Profile for a small business](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business).

## FAQ

**How much does a website for a beauty salon cost?**

With me, a one-page site is from 1,990 PLN net and a business site with up to 5 pages is 2,990 PLN net. Add 23% VAT. The domain and hosting for the first year are included.

**Should I put treatment prices on the website?**

Yes. It's the first thing clients ask. For treatments where the price depends on hair length or the size of the area, give a "from" price and say what it depends on.

**I have Instagram and Booksy. Will a website add anything?**

It gets you into Google results for specific treatments with your town's name, and gives you one place with the full price list, descriptions and directions. Booksy stays, the site just links to it. More on whether a social media profile is enough in [website or Facebook page](/en/blog/website-or-facebook-page).

**What should I prepare before ordering a website?**

A list of treatments with prices and duration, opening hours, a dozen or so photos of results and the salon, a few sentences about you and a link to your Google Business Profile. The full list is in [what to prepare for a website](/en/blog/what-to-prepare-for-a-website).
