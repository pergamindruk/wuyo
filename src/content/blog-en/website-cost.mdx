**The short answer:** in 2026 a simple business website in Poland costs 1,800–3,500 PLN, a site with several subpages 3,000–7,000 PLN, and an online shop from 5,000 PLN upwards. On top of that come running costs: from about 20 PLN a month for the domain and server.

Instead of brushing you off with "it depends", I break these numbers down: market ranges, my prices, and the things you don't see in a quote but still have to pay for. Prices are in PLN, net.

## What affects the price of a website?

- **Number of pages:** a landing page vs a large website
- **Design:** a ready template or a design from scratch
- **Features:** a contact form vs a shop, bookings, an admin panel
- **Technology:** WordPress, Next.js, Webflow, hand-written code
- **Text and photos:** whether you have them or need a copywriter and photographer
- **On-page SEO:** basic vs extensive optimisation
- **Maintenance:** one-off or with a support subscription

## What actually changes between 1,990 and 2,990 PLN

The difference between one page and a business website isn't "more screens". It's three things:

**Number of ways in from Google.** One page competes for one phrase. Five pages, each for its own. If you offer four different services, on a single page one of them will always get short shrift.

**Amount of content to write.** Five pages means five times more text that someone has to put together so it isn't filler.

**Expanding later.** A site built as one page rarely splits sensibly into subpages later. Usually it gets built a second time.

## When a cheap website makes sense

You don't always need a website for several thousand.

If you work locally, have a dozen or so jobs a month and customers find you anyway through recommendations or Google Maps, start with a well-filled-in [Google Business Profile](/en/blog/google-business-profile-small-business) and one simple page. It's a solution for a year or two, after which you'll know what you really need.

A website for several thousand makes sense when it's meant to bring real enquiries: you compete in search, sell more expensive services, or customers compare you with several businesses before calling.

If you're taking a cheap offer, check it first. I described [7 traps of a cheap website](/en/blog/cheap-website-what-to-watch-out-for) worth watching out for before paying a deposit.
