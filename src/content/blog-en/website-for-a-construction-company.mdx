## Who visits a construction company's website

Usually someone who has just bought a flat, is building a house or needs a new roof. They're on their phone, they've typed "bathroom renovation Rzeszów" or "builder Podkarpacie" into Google, and they open a few results one after another.

This person doesn't know construction. They're afraid of one thing: hiring a crew that takes the deposit and disappears. So on your site they look for proof that you're a real company and know the job:

1. Do they do what I need?
2. Do they work in my area?
3. Do they have photos of finished work and reviews from other customers?
4. How fast can I get a quote?

## A website for a construction company: what it must include

I start every site like this from this list. Not everything has to be there on day one, but each point answers a question the customer will ask on the phone anyway.

<CheckItem>A list of services. Specific ones: "turnkey bathroom renovations", "building insulation", "laying paving stones", not "construction services"</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Projects with photos. Ideally before and after, with a short note: what needed doing, the floor area, the materials</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>The area you cover. Names of towns and municipalities you work in, for example Rzeszów, Łańcut, Strzyżów</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Customer reviews, ideally pulled in from your Google Business Profile</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>Full company details: name, tax number (NIP), address. This reassures a customer who's worried about a "crew from a classified ad"</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>How working together looks: site visit, quote, contract, deposit, handover</CheckItem>
<CheckItem>A quote form and a phone number that can be tapped on a phone</CheckItem>

Each service is a good candidate for its own page. Someone searching "house insulation Rzeszów" lands straight on the insulation page, not on the home page where they have to dig for it. That's why I usually recommend a multi-page site to firms with several services. I explain the difference in [one-page or multi-page website](/en/blog/one-page-or-multi-page-website).

## Projects: phone photos are enough

This is the most important part of a construction website. Customers won't believe slogans. They'll believe photos.

You don't need a photographer. A phone and two habits will do: a photo before you start and a photo after cleaning up, from the same spot. Keep them in a separate folder named with the town and type of work. After a few months you have a ready gallery.

Two or three sentences per project are enough: where, what needed doing, how many square metres. A note like that also helps with Google, because the page then contains the town names and services people type into the search bar.

<Callout type="info">
Only show photos of other people's homes with the customer's permission. Just ask at handover. No house numbers, number plates or faces of the people who live there.
</Callout>

If you want to add new projects yourself, say once a month, WordPress with an admin panel is the better choice. If you'd rather I did it, a site built from scratch plus a care plan is enough. More on that choice in [WordPress or a custom website](/en/blog/wordpress-or-custom-website).

## A quote form instead of "please get in touch"

A plain "name, email, message" form ends with a message like "how much for a bathroom?". Then you call anyway and ask about everything.

A better form asks right away for what you need for a rough quote:

- type of work (picked from a list of your services),
- town,
- approximate floor area,
- when the customer wants to start,
- photos of the current state.

You get an enquiry that tells you whether the job suits you and whether it's worth driving out for a site visit. Fewer trips to customers who were looking for someone for a completely different job.

## FAQ

**How much does a website for a construction company cost?**

With me, a one-page site is from 1,990 PLN net and a business site with up to 5 pages is 2,990 PLN net. Add 23% VAT. The domain and hosting for the first year are included.

**I don't have project photos. Can I start without them?**

You can, but it's the weakest point of the site. Better to start with a few phone photos from recent jobs than with stock images. We add to the gallery as the jobs come in. What else to prepare is in [what to prepare for a website](/en/blog/what-to-prepare-for-a-website).

**Should I put prices on a construction company website?**

Usually not exact ones, because every job is different. A range like "from… per square metre" helps filter out enquiries that don't fit your rates. It's your call. The site can handle either.

**Is a website enough to get calls from Google?**

Not on its own. You also need separate pages for your services, town names in the text and a Google Business Profile with reviews. A site with just a slogan and a phone number barely shows up in search results.
