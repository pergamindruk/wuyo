Choosing a logo designer is one of the more important decisions for your brand. A bad choice costs you time, money and nerves. This guide will help you choose wisely.

## 8 questions you must ask a designer

<Callout type="info">
Ask these questions before you sign a contract or transfer a deposit. The answers will tell you more than the portfolio.
</Callout>

**1. Can I see logo designs for businesses similar to mine?**

A good portfolio has varied projects, but the designer should understand the specifics of different industries. A law firm needs a different logo than a food truck.

**2. What does your design process look like?**

A professional has a clear process: brief → research → sketches → digital design → presentation → revisions → final files. No process means no experience.

**3. How many logo options and revisions are included in the price?**

Check exactly what you get: 1 proposal or 3 directions? How many rounds of revisions? What happens when they run out?

**4. What files will I get and in what formats?**

You must get: SVG or AI (vector), PNG with a transparent background, a print PDF. Without vectors you can't print the logo on a shop sign or banner.

**5. Is the copyright transferred in the price?**

This is key. Without a full transfer of rights you can't register the logo as a trademark or use it freely. Ask directly, and check the contract.

**6. How long does it take?**

A realistic time for a logo: 5–14 working days. A designer promising "ready in 48 hours" either has a ready-made template or isn't sleeping for two days.

**7. Do you have reviews from previous clients?**

Ask for contact with 2–3 previous clients or check Google Maps / Trustpilot. No reviews is a yellow flag.

**8. What if I don't like the design?**

Ask about the revision policy and what happens if you're not satisfied. A good designer has clear rules and doesn't leave the client with nothing.

## Where to look for a logo designer

**Recommended places:**
- Behance.net: portfolios of designers from around the world
- Dribbble.com: top designers, higher prices
- LinkedIn: direct contact with freelancers
- Facebook groups for freelancers
- Recommendations from people you know: the best way

**Avoid:**
- "$5 logo" platforms (Fiverr under 200 PLN): there you buy templates
- Logo contests (99designs): designers work without a guarantee of payment = low quality
- AI logo generators: nothing unique, copyright problems

## Red flags: run from this designer

<Callout type="warning">
**Warning signs:**
- Doesn't ask about your business, goes straight to colours and "style"
- A portfolio in one style (every logo looks the same)
- No information about the process or deadlines
- A price under 300 PLN for a "professional logo"
- Won't sign a contract
- Final files only in JPG/PNG
- The copyright "stays with the designer"
</Callout>

## FAQ

**How do I judge a logo designer's portfolio?**

Check whether the projects look varied (different styles, industries): it's a sign the designer adapts to the client rather than repeating their own style. Check whether the logos work at a small size (e.g. as a favicon) and in black and white.

**Is it worth paying more for an experienced designer?**

Yes. An experienced designer doesn't just make a nice logo: they understand how a mark behaves in print, on screen and on a shop sign. A higher cost upfront means lower costs for revisions and redesigns later.

**What if I don't have the budget for a professional?**

Start with Canva or an AI logo, but treat it as a temporary solution. When the business starts earning, order a logo from a designer. Redesigning from a Canva starting point costs around 1,000–2,000 PLN.
